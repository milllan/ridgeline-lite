The Front Range sits in the heart of "Hail Alley." Between May and September, storms rolling off the foothills drop hail on the Denver metro more often than almost anywhere else in the country — and wind gusts that peel shingles off in sheets. When it happens to your roof, two clocks start ticking: one on water damage, one on your insurance filing window.

## What to do in the first 48 hours

Get the roof tarped if it's actively leaking — that's what our 24/7 storm line is for. Then document everything: date of the storm, photos of hail on the ground, dents in gutters or AC fins, and any interior stains. Don't climb the roof yourself, and be wary of out-of-state crews knocking doors the morning after; Colorado law gives you the right to cancel storm-chaser contracts, but it's easier not to sign one.

## Honest assessments, documented properly

Not every storm totals a roof, and we won't tell you it did. Our inspectors chalk-mark and photograph functional damage — fractured mats, exposed fiberglass, creased tabs — the same evidence categories adjusters are trained on. If the damage is cosmetic or below claim thresholds, we'll say so and quote the spot repairs instead. When a claim is warranted, that documentation becomes your leverage: we meet the adjuster on the roof and walk them through it strike by strike.

## Restoration, not just replacement

Hail rarely stops at shingles. Our restoration scope covers gutters and downspouts, ridge vents, pipe boots, skylight flashing, window screens, and painted surfaces — the items adjusters legitimately pay for but homeowners forget to claim. Most restorations complete within two weeks of claim approval, and every one carries our 10-year workmanship warranty on top of the manufacturer's coverage.
